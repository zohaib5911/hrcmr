## Make the check part of onboarding

Check permission to do the proposed work before employment begins. Apply a consistent process to every recruit; do not use appearance, accent or nationality as a shortcut. Allocate a trained checker and a deputy so a busy start date does not bypass the check.

## Choose the correct route

Use the Home Office employer online service where appropriate, an eligible digital identity check, or the prescribed original-document process. A worker showing you their own immigration account is not the employer online check. Match the person to the evidence and check restrictions against the actual role and hours.

## Leave an evidence trail

Record who checked, when, the method and the retained evidence. Restrict access to these records. Where permission is time-limited, assign the follow-up task immediately. Use the Employer Checking Service where the official guidance directs you to it; an application receipt alone is not a completed check.

## A practical handover

Before confirming readiness, ask the checker to show the evidence and any outstanding restrictions to the person authorising the start. Give unresolved cases a named owner and seek qualified advice rather than guessing.
